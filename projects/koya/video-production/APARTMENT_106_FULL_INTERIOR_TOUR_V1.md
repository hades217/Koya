## Deliverable strategy

Apartment 106 needs two sales cuts rather than forcing one duration to do two jobs:

1. **Cinematic highlight cut - approximately 36 seconds**: existing 0-21 seconds plus kitchen, invitation, living and terrace payoff. This sells the emotional idea quickly.
2. **Complete apartment tour - approximately 78 seconds**: existing 0-21 seconds plus every interior space in a physically legible route. This answers buyer questions and supports serious consideration.

The complete tour is not a montage. One camera follows the same resident through every doorway, visibly enters and exits each selected room and finishes at the strongest public-space payoff.

## Recommended physical route

Corrected route ledger, formal audit and floor-plan overlay: `APARTMENT_106_ROUTE_LEDGER_V2.md`, `APARTMENT_106_FLOORPLAN_AUDIT_V1.md` and `../production-assets/contact-sheets/apartment-106-literal-route-v2.png`.

The route starts from the actual 21-second frame with camera and resident already inside the entry hall.

```text
Entry/hall
  -> Main bathroom and adjacent laundry
  -> Bedroom 2 (enter, reveal, return through same door)
  -> Continue east along the hall/kitchen axis
  -> MPR (enter from the kitchen/dining side, reveal compact flexible use, return)
  -> Bedroom 1
  -> Walk-in robe
  -> Ensuite
  -> Return through Bedroom 1
  -> Kitchen galley
  -> Clear island end and turn to dining
  -> Invitation beat
  -> Living
  -> Terrace payoff
```

This order deliberately finishes on living and terrace. It does not end in a bathroom, laundry or bedroom.

## Timed full-tour beat sheet

### 21-24s - Entry orientation

- Preserve the actual 21-second frame and momentum.
- Resident slows slightly and indicates the private side of the apartment with a restrained gesture.
- Camera remains behind her; no presenter-style dialogue.

### 24-29s - Main bathroom and laundry

- The bathroom and laundry are encountered from the entry/hall side before the route reaches Bedroom 2.
- Main bathroom door opens to reveal shower, vanity and toilet at believable scale.
- Laundry adjacency remains visible from the hall; do not combine them into one invented luxury bathroom.
- Camera may enter the bathroom threshold but should not waste time circling fixtures.

### 29-35s - Bedroom 2

- Resident opens the real Bedroom 2 door and enters first.
- Camera physically crosses the doorway and arcs only far enough to show bed, robe and external light relationship.
- Resident stays near the room edge, leaving the floor path readable.
- Camera and resident exit through the same door; no wall wipe.

### 35-39s - Hall to kitchen axis

- Camera and resident return through the Bedroom 2 door and regain the same hall.
- Continue east into the kitchen circulation line; no hidden relocation behind a door or body.
- The MPR opening only becomes available from the kitchen/dining side shown on the Type 106 plan.

### 39-44s - MPR

- Show the MPR as compact and genuinely multipurpose, not a third oversized bedroom.
- Suggested staging: quiet study / reading / occasional guest function with built-in desk and compact daybed.
- Camera crosses the doorway or opening, pauses briefly, then returns to the hall.

### 44-49s - Kitchen passage and Bedroom 1 approach

- Continue east along the real galley route.
- Continuous cabinetry remains camera-left and the single island remains camera-right.
- Use close parallax on timber, stone and tapware to show material quality without blocking the resident.

### 49-55s - Bedroom 1

- Resident opens and crosses the Bedroom 1 door; camera follows.
- Establish bed, glazing/terrace relationship and the entry to the walk-in robe.
- Normal residential scale; no hotel suite enlargement.

### 55-61s - Walk-in robe and ensuite

- Follow the actual connected suite sequence.
- Show the robe as a circulation space, then reveal the ensuite materials.
- Camera remains physically inside the same suite and exits back through Bedroom 1.

### 61-66s - Return to public zone

- Resident and camera leave Bedroom 1 through the same door.
- Kitchen, dining light and terrace reappear progressively.
- This is the emotional transition from private inspection to home reveal.

### 66-71s - Dining and invitation

- Both visibly clear the island end before turning.
- Resident moves to the left third, turns back about 35 degrees, gives warm eye contact and an understated open-right-hand invitation.
- Bottle remains in her left hand.

### 71-78s - Living and terrace payoff

- Camera accepts the invitation and moves past her into living.
- Dining remains readable on the right, living opens left/centre and terrace glazing becomes the destination.
- Continue toward or just across the terrace threshold depending on the final approved terrace concept.
- End with gentle forward movement, exterior detail retained and no frozen still.

## Character and performance arc

- Detailed performance source: `APARTMENT_106_CHARACTER_PERFORMANCE_V1.md`.
- Candidate expression/gesture board: `../production-assets/assets/generated/character/sports-female/resident-sports-female-performance-board-v1.png`.
- The resident is not an always-forward-moving NPC.
- In private rooms she steps aside and lets the camera inspect rather than walking through furniture.
- At room exits she re-establishes the next route with body orientation, not sudden teleportation.
- Only one direct acknowledgement occurs: the invitation before living. Repeating smiles and gestures in every room would feel like a property presenter.
- Bottle stays in the left hand throughout unless a later approved action visibly places it on a surface and retrieves it.
- Natural breathing, irregular blinking, heel-to-toe weight transfer, restrained bottle inertia and ponytail lag must remain visible.
- The invitation uses 0.8-1.2 seconds of warm eye contact, an emerging half-smile and one open-right-palm movement; no speech or lip-sync.

## Camera grammar

- 35-40mm equivalent lens, approximately 1.55-1.60m height.
- Straight passages use restrained Steadicam forward motion.
- Rooms use small physical arcs from the doorway, not impossible wide sweeps through walls.
- Every exit reuses the same door just entered unless the floor plan proves another connection.
- No room gets a full 360-degree spin; orientation remains understandable in reverse.
- Doors, bodies and furniture may create parallax but may never hide a location swap.

## Sound chapters

- Bedrooms: softer footsteps, fabric and curtain movement, reduced city sound.
- MPR: quiet room tone; no fake office typing or lifestyle action required.
- Bathroom/laundry: tighter tile reflection and subtle ventilation tone.
- Kitchen: timber-floor steps, restrained bottle movement and wider open-plan reverb.
- Living/terrace: distant Brisbane ambience, light foliage/curtain movement and the warm musical motif.

## Seedance production packaging

Seedance 2.5 cannot create a 57-second interior continuation in one task. After all new room anchors are approved, divide the interior into three reference-locked legs while preserving visual continuity:

1. **Interior Leg 1 - approximately 20 seconds**: 21s start -> bathroom/laundry -> Bedroom 2 -> same-door exit -> kitchen-axis approach -> MPR entry.
2. **Interior Leg 2 - approximately 20 seconds**: MPR reveal and same-door exit -> kitchen -> Bedroom 1 -> WIR -> ensuite -> begin return.
3. **Interior Leg 3 - approximately 17 seconds**: return through WIR and Bedroom 1 -> kitchen -> physical island clearance -> invitation -> living -> terrace.

Every next leg must use the actual returned last frame of the accepted previous leg as its first frame. No paid leg is submitted until its exact references, prompt, parameters and cost estimate are approved.

## Rejection conditions

- Any listed room is skipped or replaced by a montage cut.
- Camera enters a room but exits through a wall or different unproven doorway.
- MPR grows into a large bedroom or living room.
- Bathroom and laundry merge into an invented room.
- Bedroom 1, WIR and ensuite lose their plan relationship.
- Island changes side, duplicates or hides a room replacement.
- Character, wardrobe or bottle hand changes.
- Repeated commercial smiles, long lens-staring, mannequin pauses or game-like walk cycles.
- Hands, feet, hair or bottle intersect architecture or lose natural inertia.
- Daylight changes into evening/night.
- Any concept is described as a developer-approved Apartment 106 render.
